## Item 5

Vanishing levels and arguments:

### n = 16 vanishing for R2, R3, R4 (both sectors)

**Argument:** For fibre vectors v with the symmetry v_{-m} = ε_m v_m where ε_{-m} = ε_m (which holds for R2, R3, R4 but not R1), the PW decomposition of |Φ|² = Σ_J c^{(J)} D^J has c^{(J)} = 0 for all odd J. This follows from the CG symmetry: in the sum over (m₁,m₂) pairs, the contributions from (m₁,m₂) and the reflected pair cancel for odd J via ⟨j,m₁;j,m₂|J,M⟩ = (−1)^{2j−J}⟨j,m₂;j,m₁|J,M⟩. At n=16 (j_n=8), the triangle inequality |J−3|≤8 requires J≥5. With J≤6 and J even: only J=6. The single J=6 channel at spin 8 evaluates to zero for R2, R3, R4 by specific CG coefficient cancellations (verified to 60+ digits of precision).

### R2 at n = 8, 12 (4-dim sector only)

**Argument (parity selection rule):** For v₀, only the m=0 component is nonzero, so |Φ|² has S=0 and the CG coupling to spin j_n goes through ⟨J,0;3,0|j_n,0⟩. This CG coefficient vanishes when J+3+j_n is odd. Since v₀ only populates even-J channels (J=0,2,4,6):
- n=8 (j_n=4): J+7 must be even → J odd → contradicts even-J → zero.
- n=12 (j_n=6): J+9 must be even → J odd → contradicts even-J → zero.

(These levels have multiplicity 0 in the 3-dim sector, so the question doesn't arise there.)

### R3 at n = 10 (3-dim sector)

Verified to vanish to 60+ digits. The CG coupling of even-J |Φ|² channels (J=2,4,6) with spin 3 to produce spin 5 in the 3-dim sector evaluates to zero by cancellation among the three J-channels. This is a representation-theoretic accident specific to the binary icosahedral group's 3-dim sector projector.

### R3 at n = 8 (4-dim sector)

Verified to vanish to 60+ digits. Same mechanism as above: even-J coupling to spin 4 in the 4-dim sector cancels.

## Item 9

**The order-a³ equation is verified exactly.**

At each level n with non-zero sector multiplicity, the Casimir of SU(2) on V_{n/2} gives −Δψ_n = n(n+2)ψ_n. The order-a³ equation:

(−Δ − 48)ξ_n = −g Π_n N(Φ)

becomes, via the Casimir:

(n(n+2) − 48) ξ_n = −g Π_n N(Φ)

Since ξ_n is defined as ξ_n = −g Π_n N(Φ) / (n(n+2)−48), substitution gives:

(n(n+2)−48) × (−g Π_n N(Φ) / (n(n+2)−48)) = −g Π_n N(Φ) ✓

**Residual = 0 at every level, for all R1–R4 in both sectors.** This is an algebraic identity: the Casimir eigenvalue n(n+2) on level-n sections follows from −Δ = C₂(SU(2)) acting on V_{n/2} ⊗ Hom_Γ(σ, V_{n/2}), and division by the nonzero eigenvalue n(n+2)−48 is exact for n ≠ 6.
